# Fight Night at the Ruy Lopez Arena

Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to Fight Night. Two fighters, sixty-four squares, no referee stoppages except the ones we call ourselves. Tonight's card: the Ruy Lopez, the oldest grudge match in chess. In the white corner, a queen who fights like a debt collector, so we're calling her White-Queen-The-Repo-Man. In the black corner, a rook that never stopped throwing punches: Black-Rook-Rocky. Ring the bell.

1. e4

The classic opening jab. Nothing fancy, nothing wrong. Center, space, tempo.

3. Bb5

The Ruy Lopez proper. White puts the question to the c6 knight before the crowd has finished sitting down. This is book, and book is good.

4. Ba4

White takes the bishop back a step after 3... a6. Solid, mainline, the sort of move you make with your eyes closed.

6. Bxc6

And here is the first eyebrow. White retreated to a4 last move only to trade the bishop off anyway. The Exchange Variation is a fine idea, but taking it on move four saves a full tempo. Call it a wasted round of footwork. Meet White-Bishop-Indecisive, who left the ring early.

10. Be3

Development, but the bishop walks straight into the path of a knight that is about to say hello. Remember this square.

12. Ne2

White regroups the knight toward the kingside. Slow, but with a plan: Ng3 and Nf5 to kick at the black bishop.

15... Bxe3

Black cashes in. Bishop for bishop, and White's pawn structure takes the hit: the f-pawn recaptures and the king's cover is now a little draughty. Black-Bishop-The-Surgeon did one thing all night and did it cleanly.

17... Bxf5

Black trades the other bishop for the knight that had been squatting on f5. Two minor pieces gone for two minor pieces. Material is level, but hold on.

19... Qxf5

Black picks up the loose f5 pawn. One pawn ahead, and the crowd smells blood.

20. Qc4+

Check! And this is the counterpunch nobody saw. The check forces Black to answer, and the knight on c5 is left hanging like a chin.

21. Qxc5

The Repo Man collects. Knight for a pawn. White is now a clean two points up and the momentum has flipped completely. If you want the turning point of this fight, it is right here, in the two moves from 20 to 21. Black's 20... Qe6 blocked the check but forgot the knight.

22. Qc4+

Another check. White-Queen-The-Repo-Man is doing laps.

27... Qxb3

Black offers the queen trade and White accepts. With a knight to the good, trading queens is exactly what White wants. Black-Queen-Retired heads to the showers.

29. d4

White opens the center. Sensible: more open lines favor the side with the extra piece.

33... bxa3

En passant! Black finds the sneaky capture and starts chipping at White's pawns. This is where Rocky gets up off the stool.

35... Rxe3

Rocky takes a pawn. White's extra knight is still there, but the pawn count is starting to tell a different story.

36... Rxb3

And another! Material is dead level again: White's extra knight is now bought and paid for by three extra black pawns. White has let a winning position slide into a scrap. If White-Knight-The-Wanderer is going to earn his money, this is the moment.

38. Na7

The Wanderer goes to a7, which is the sort of square where knights go to be forgotten. It is on the rim, it is far from the kingside, and Rocky is loose in White's half.

42... c4

Black starts pushing the passed c-pawn. With the knight stranded, this pawn is the whole fight for Black.

46... Rd2+

Check on the second rank. Rocky has White's king running. Kg3, Rd3+, Kg2, Rd2+, Kg1: three checks, and White is happy to take them, because repeating the position is a draw, and a draw was not what Black wanted a few moves ago.

50. Re3

White finally breaks the check loop and puts a rook on the third rank. Two rooks and a knight against two rooks and three extra pawns. White has the piece, Black has a passed c-pawn on c4 backed by another on c5, and rooks that will not sit down.

52. Nc3

The Wanderer comes home! After ten moves in exile on a7 and b5, the knight lands on c3, right in front of Black's passed pawn. This is the move White needed twenty moves ago, but late is better than never.

53... Rh2

Rocky slides over to the h-file, hunting the h3 pawn and keeping the king boxed in. Active, but the c-pawn is now the only thing Black really has, and the rook has left it behind.

56... Rd8

Black's second rook, Black-Rook-The-Understudy, has spent the whole night watching from the back rank. It steps up now, but the c5 pawn is unguarded, and the Repo Man's old firm is still collecting.

57. Rxc5

White takes the c5 pawn. One passed pawn left for Black, and it is now facing a knight and a rook. The tide is turning back to White.

58... g5+

Check with the pawn, chasing White's king up the board. It looks scary, but the king strolls to h5 and then h6, and suddenly White-King-Ironman is standing next to Black's king with a smile.

60... c3

Black pushes the last passed pawn one square from glory. Brave, but the pawn is walking into a crowd.

61. Rexc3

White takes the pawn. Black recaptures with the rook, and White recaptures again.

62. Rxc3

Smoke clears: rook and knight against a lone rook, pawns roughly even. White is a full knight ahead with the king already camped on h6. Black-Rook-Rocky fought the whole card, but the Understudy's late entrance cost the c-pawn and the fight.

62... Kg8

The black king steps out of the corner. This is where the board stood when the broadcast went out; the game is still being played.

# Scorecards

Scorecards so far. White: an early tempo dropped in the opening, a beautiful two-move combination to win a knight, a slow leak of pawns that let a won game get messy, and then a patient recovery once the knight came home. Grade B. Black: a lost knight to a queen check that should never have been allowed, relentless rook play that clawed the material all the way back, and then a passed pawn pushed into a crowd. Grade B minus, for heart. Piece of the night: Black-Rook-Rocky. Comeback of the night: White-Knight-The-Wanderer, from a7 to the winning c3 square. We will see you for the final bell.
